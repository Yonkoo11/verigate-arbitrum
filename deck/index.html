The problem

A tokenized stock is a security.
It can't move to the wrong hands.

Robinhood Chain exists to host tokenized US equities — TSLA, AMZN. By law a tokenized share cannot transfer to an un-KYC'd wallet, a sanctioned jurisdiction, or (often) a non-accredited investor.

The chain ships the assets. Every builder on its $1M developer fund still has to solve compliance — and static allow-lists break the moment a token touches secondary trading or DeFi.

CV Covenant02
The solution

The compliance layer for tokenized stocks.

Covenant enforces KYC, jurisdiction, and accreditation rules at the token itself. Every transfer is checked against on-chain KYC attestations before it can settle.

Modular

One ComplianceEngine, pluggable rules: jurisdiction, accreditation, holder cap. ERC-3643-aligned.

EAS-compatible

Reads canonical EAS on Arbitrum One, or a bundled attester on chains where EAS isn't deployed yet — same code.

How it works · the same transfer, two recipients

Valid KYC in the wrong jurisdiction is still no transfer.

Without verification
Transfer reverted
Recipient holds no KYC attestation — the token refuses to move. REVERTED · CountryRestriction
With verification
Transfer settles
Jurisdiction, accreditation, holder cap all pass — the share moves on-chain.

That jurisdiction check is the part address allow-lists can't express.
